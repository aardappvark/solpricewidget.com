3. How Geographic Screening Works

Sol Price Widget uses a multi-signal approach to determine the user’s geographic location. This screening occurs at app startup and uses the following detection methods in priority order:

PriorityMethodData sourceReliability
1SIM card country codeTelephonyManager (SIM operator)Highest — physical SIM issued in a specific country
2Network carrier countryTelephonyManager (network operator)High — carrier-reported country code
3Device locale settingAndroid system localeModerate — user-configurable but indicative

3.1 Privacy-preserving design

  • All screening is performed entirely on-device — no location data is transmitted to any server
  • The App does not request location permissions (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION or ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION)
  • No location history is stored, logged, or recorded
  • Country detection relies solely on SIM, carrier, and locale signals already available to the app
  • See our Privacy Policy for full details on data handling

3.2 What happens when a restricted region is detected

If any detection signal indicates the device is associated with a restricted jurisdiction:

  1. The App displays an informational compliance screen explaining that Sol Price Widget is not available in the detected region
  2. The screen identifies the detected region for transparency
  3. Access to the App’s features (price display, widget, settings) is fully restricted
  4. The back button is disabled to prevent bypass
  5. No data is collected, transmitted, or processed beyond the initial region check

3.3 Limitations of geographic screening

We acknowledge that on-device geographic screening has inherent limitations:

  • SIM-based detection may not reflect the user’s current physical location (e.g., travelling with a foreign SIM)
  • Locale-based detection can be changed by the user in device settings
  • VPNs or location spoofing tools may circumvent geographic restrictions
  • Dual-SIM devices may report ambiguous country information

These limitations do not relieve users of their obligation to comply with applicable laws in their actual jurisdiction (see Section 4).
